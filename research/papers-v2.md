### What the adaptations measure

- A five-basis-point bin is defined relative to each session’s opening price. Each of the first 30 bars contributes one count to every bin its high-to-low span intersects. This approximates time at price without a tape of individual transactions.
- The label uses a one-share hypothetical trade with 5 basis points of friction on each side. The activity paper’s label, futures contract, position rules and returns belong to its original experiment. The SPY adaptation uses the same next known entry clock as the rules.
- Every model fits and scales on past resolved sessions before the test month. The recent-only and transferred networks share a scaler learned from the recent training window. For transfer, all source labels predate that window. Two-stage training changes both the exposure to older observations and the training trajectory, which is why the full-history single-stage control is important.
- Brier error is the mean squared gap between a predicted probability and the realised 0/1 outcome. Small values are better. Comparisons use matched days, with paired 10-observation block intervals and a floor of 60 matched observations. The agreement subset contributes only 23 days in the archived run. Intervals are exploratory and unadjusted for multiple comparisons.
- Neural forecasting, reviewer commentary, and the numeric fill reconciliation have separate entry points. None supplies a Boolean flag to the paper runner.
